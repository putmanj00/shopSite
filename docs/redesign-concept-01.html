Concept 01 · design system & rationale

The Journal, Opened

The current site stacks centered sections on parchment and lets CRO widgets shout over a quiet brand. This concept commits fully to the one idea nobody else in the category owns — the store is a field journal. Every device follows: products are numbered entries, collections are plates, filters are the index, provenance is the spec sheet, and the dark “Deep Woods” register finally gets a job. Same stack, same components, new clothes.

What changed, and why

Palette — inherited, re-weighted

Two derived ink shades, no new hues. The change is proportion: gold moves from decoration to information — as rules and borders at full strength, and as a darkened ink gold wherever it becomes text on light grounds (raw #C9A642 measures 1.99:1 on parchment; ink gold holds ≥4.6:1). Terracotta narrows to actions and the italic display clause, and forest gets the whole Deep Woods register instead of a footer cameo.

The current h1 tops out at 48px — timid for a serif with this much drawing in it. Cormorant at 90–105px, weight 500, is the concept's one deliberate extravagance. Everything else stays quiet to afford it.

Signature devices

The plate frameDouble rule — 1px ink outer, 1px gold inset at 7px. Applied to every image, echoing the museum-plate illustrations. One CSS class (.plate), replaces card borders, shadows stay whisper-quiet.
Entry numbersEvery product gets a sequential journal №, displayed in ink-gold caps. Provenance, scarcity, and collection-hunting (“what number are you?”) in one device. Stored as a Shopify metafield. Governance: one counter, never renumber, gaps are fine; Shopify enforces no uniqueness, so assignment is discipline. Numbering starts honest — the store holds 2 products today — or backfills legitimately from the IG archive (235 posts since 2021). №s in this mockup are illustrative.
Ruled journal lines34–43px repeating baseline rules at low opacity behind hero and photo briefs — the page reads as paper without a single skeuomorphic texture file.
Photo treatment lawProducts: natural window light on parchment sweep (Open Field). Premium: single warm key on dark slate (Deep Woods). Portraits: makers mid-work, honest clutter. No stock, ever — enforced by the existing slop-detector gate.

Motion

Three moments only, all honoring prefers-reduced-motion: (1) the catalog strip drifts at reading pace; (2) plates lift 2px with the gold inset brightening on hover; (3) the PDP figure crossfades 250ms on thumbnail select. No scroll-jacking, no parallax, no entrance choreography — framer-motion is already installed and this is all it should do.

Mobile

Thumb chromeUnder 960px the desktop nav folds into a hamburger drawer (Search first) and a fixed bottom tab bar takes over: Home / Shop / Booth / Cart — every tab a ≥48px touch target, and Search stays in the header at all widths. Booth earns a permanent thumb slot because the markets→online funnel is the business lever. Maps onto the existing mobile-bottom-nav.tsx.
Sticky buy barThe PDP pins price + add-to-cart above the iOS safe-area inset (env(safe-area-inset-bottom)), so the buy decision never scrolls away while reading field notes — the existing sticky-add-to-cart.tsx, restyled to the plate language. Design-note pins collapse to inline margin notes on small screens.

Removed, deliberately

  • Welcome popup — mounted today at layout.tsx:140–141 (with exit-intent); replaced by the permanent Inner Circle + market composition. Exit-intent survives as fallback.
  • Countdown component — never mounted on the live site; delete the dormant cro/countdown-timer.tsx before anyone mounts it. Fabricated urgency contradicts “made slowly, meant to last.”
  • Dead discount-code input — cart-drawer.tsx:63 throws “coming soon” in live UI; erodes trust, returns when the feature exists.
  • Trust-badge iconography — folded into the hero proof line and the Deep Woods promise band, in words.
  • Review sign-in wall — the wall is UI-only (review-form.tsx:25); the server API is already open and auto-approves. The real work is review storage + email verification + rate limiting, not the wall.
